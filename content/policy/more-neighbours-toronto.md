I founded More Neighbours Toronto in June 2021, not long after coming home from San Francisco, because the housing debate in Toronto had plenty of people showing up to say no and almost no one organized to say yes. The idea was simple: a volunteer group that turns up at council, at community meetings and at Queen's Park to argue for more homes, in every neighbourhood, for the people who aren't in the room yet.

It grew into the largest grassroots housing-abundance organization in the country, with hundreds of active volunteers and a flat, non-partisan structure that welcomes people from across the political spectrum. Along the way it:

- was consulted by Ontario's Housing Affordability Task Force, whose 2022 report reset the province's housing conversation;
- became a registered third-party advertiser and endorsed pro-housing council candidates in the 2022 Toronto election;
- ranked the parties' housing plans during the 2025 provincial election;
- co-published a Citizen Developer's Guide in 2026, so ordinary owners can add homes themselves;
- became a driving force in Toronto's zoning reform, from multiplexes to apartments on major streets.

I stepped away from the organization when I ran for the Ontario Liberal leadership, and I'm no longer part of it. It is in good hands, with an elected board, and it still needs volunteers. If you care about housing in Toronto, go and join them.
