Ontario Liberal leadership, 2026

A province-wide leadership campaign built on economic growth and government capability, with the race's first fully costed platform: thirteen policy areas and more than 190 commitments.

Candidate for Leader · January to September 2026

Explore the platform ↗Platform PDF ↗

In January 2026 I wrote in The Hub that everyone in Ontario politics seemed to be managing decline, and that I was exploring a run for the leadership of the Ontario Liberal Party to fight for growth instead. In June, after conversations with more than 1,500 people in 35 ridings, I formally entered the race under the banner "Own the Future." On September 30, 2026 I withdrew and returned to practice.

The campaign was built on a simple argument: Ontario's living standards have stalled, young people are paying for it, and the province needs a government that can actually build. We ran it as an outsider campaign with a small team, strong digital work, and more policy than anyone else in the race.

How it unfolded

  • January 14, 2026. Bonnie Crombie resigns as leader and the race opens.
  • January 29. I publish the exploratory piece, "Everyone's managing decline," in The Hub, and start touring ridings.
  • June 9. I register and formally enter the race, the first candidate to release a fully costed platform.
  • August 10 and September 17. The party's leadership debates.
  • September 2. Conservative MP Matt Strauss joins the Ontario Liberals to back the campaign.
  • September 30. I withdraw from the race and endorse Lee Fairclough. Members vote online November 9 to 20, with the result on November 21, 2026.

Why I stepped back

I was honest about it at the time: as a young candidate who had never held elected office, I was a tough sell to a membership that wanted a proven winner, and I was not willing to finish the race in debt. The ideas got a fair hearing. The question the membership kept asking was about readiness, and that is a question I intend to answer over time, not in a single contest.

What the campaign left behind

A clearer public case for a growth agenda in Ontario, a body of policy that others are welcome to borrow from, a network of people across the province who want the same things, and a long list of interviews, columns and coverage, archived below.

I'm grateful to everyone who signed up, volunteered, donated, hosted an event, or simply took the argument seriously. The work continues here.
